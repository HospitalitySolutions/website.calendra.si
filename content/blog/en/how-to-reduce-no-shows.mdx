Every no-show leaves a slot that is often difficult to refill at the last minute. As a simple hypothetical example, an average service value of 35 EUR and three missed appointments a week represents 5,460 EUR of unfilled service value per year for one provider. The actual business loss depends on margin, whether the slot can be refilled and other costs.

People miss appointments for many reasons. Forgetfulness is one, but published research also points to organisational and personal barriers. A better goal is therefore to build a process that helps clients remember, reschedule or cancel in time rather than assume there is one universal fix.

## What the evidence actually says about reminders

The strongest published evidence comes from healthcare rather than beauty salons. A Cochrane review found that mobile-message reminders improve attendance compared with no reminder, and a systematic review of SMS appointment reminders also found improved healthcare attendance.

That supports using reminders, but it does not justify promising that a salon will cut no-shows by a specific percentage. Industry, customer base, booking lead time, cancellation process and message design can all change the result.

## 1. Send a written confirmation after booking

Immediately give the client the date, time, service, location and provider where relevant. A written confirmation is useful even when the booking was created by phone, because the customer has a record they can check later.

## 2. Automate the reminder and test the timing

There is no universal rule that every reminder must be sent exactly 24 hours ahead. A randomised study of more than 54,000 healthcare appointments found that the number and timing of reminders can matter, including a combination of reminders at different points before the visit.

For a service business, start from your cancellation window. Send the reminder early enough that a client can change plans and you still have a realistic chance to offer the slot to someone else. Then compare no-shows and timely cancellations before and after the change.

SMS can work well for short, time-sensitive messages, while email is useful for longer confirmations and instructions. See [SMS or email for appointment reminders](/en/blog/sms-vs-email-appointment-reminders) for the channel trade-offs.

## 3. Make cancellation or rescheduling easy

A reminder is more useful if the customer immediately knows what to do when their plans change. Include a clear cancellation or rescheduling route — for example a link, button or short instruction.

A timely cancellation is not the same as a no-show. If you learn about it early enough, you can offer the slot to another client or to someone on a waiting list.

## 4. State the cancellation policy before booking

Keep the rule short, understandable and visible before the appointment is confirmed. Explain the free cancellation window and what happens after it.

We would not claim that a policy alone reduces no-shows. Its main value is setting expectations clearly and giving the business a consistent rule to apply.

## When to consider a deposit

A deposit or online payment is a commercial policy, not a guaranteed no-show intervention. Consider it in relation to appointment value and duration, limited capacity and the client's previous booking behaviour.

| Example | Possible approach |
| --- | --- |
| Short appointment for a regular client | No deposit |
| Long or high-value service | Partial deposit or online payment |
| Group service with limited capacity | Payment at booking may be appropriate |
| Repeated no-shows by the same client | Stricter terms for future bookings |

Before introducing deposits, confirm refund, invoicing and tax treatment with your accountant.

## Measure no-shows and timely cancellations separately

After introducing reminders, track at least three figures:

1. no-shows without notice,
2. cancellations early enough for the slot to be re-offered,
3. the share of cancelled slots that are actually refilled.

That tells you whether the process is reducing lost capacity. Even if total cancellations increase, the business result may improve when silent no-shows become timely cancellations.
