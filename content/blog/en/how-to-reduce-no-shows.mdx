Every no-show costs you twice. You lose the revenue from that appointment, and you lose the chance to give the slot to another customer who would gladly have taken it. At an average service price of 35 EUR and three no-shows a week, that is roughly 5,400 EUR a year for a single employee.

The good news is that no-shows are almost never malicious. The most common cause is simple forgetfulness. The second most common is that the customer did not know how to cancel or reschedule, so they did nothing at all.

## Why customers forget appointments

An appointment booked three weeks ahead rarely makes it into the customer's own calendar. When they book by phone, you write it down, not them. If they receive nothing in writing afterwards, the appointment exists only in your system.

The second cause is friction around cancelling. If the only way to cancel is a phone call during business hours, some customers will simply stay home. Calling to say you are not coming feels awkward to many people. Silence is easier.

The third cause is lead time. An appointment made a month ahead has a substantially higher no-show rate than one made for next week. Same-week bookings almost always show up.

## Four measures that actually work

### 1. An immediate written confirmation

The moment a booking is made, the customer should receive a message with the service, date, time, provider name and the address. This is not just courtesy. The message is a record they can find later, and the first moment they can notice they picked the wrong date.

In [Calendra](/en/booking), the confirmation is sent automatically for every booking, whether it came through online booking or you entered it yourself over the phone.

### 2. An SMS reminder 24 hours ahead

A reminder the day before is the single most effective measure. Twenty-four hours is early enough for the customer to reschedule and late enough that the appointment stays in mind.

For reminders, SMS is more reliable than email. Email lands in a promotions tab or goes unread, while an SMS appears on the lock screen. If you send both, use email for the confirmation and SMS for the reminder.

For services booked several weeks ahead, add a second reminder a week before. For appointments booked less than 24 hours ahead, no reminder is needed.

### 3. A reschedule link in every message

This is the measure businesses most often skip. Every message, both the confirmation and the reminder, should contain a link that lets the customer reschedule or cancel in two clicks, with no phone call and no login.

A cancellation two days out is not a loss. It is information you can act on by offering the slot to someone else. A silent no-show is a loss. So the goal is not to prevent cancellations, it is to prevent silence.

### 4. A clear cancellation policy, stated up front

Keep the policy short and always visible: until when cancellation is free, and what applies after that. Put it on the booking page and in the confirmation message.

The mere fact that a policy exists, and that the customer sees it while booking, changes behaviour, even if you rarely enforce it in practice.

## When to require a deposit

A deposit is the strongest tool against no-shows and also the riskiest for the customer relationship. Use it selectively rather than across the board.

| Situation | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Regular customer, short service | No deposit |
| New customer, service over 60 minutes | Deposit or card hold |
| Customer with a history of no-shows | Deposit |
| Group session with limited places | Full payment up front |
| Service with expensive materials | Partial deposit |

A partial deposit of around 20 percent is usually enough. The amount is small enough not to deter the booking and large enough to remind the customer that the appointment is a real commitment.

## What to do when someone still does not show

Some no-shows will always happen. What you do next determines whether it happens again.

Send a short, neutral message the same day: you noticed they missed the appointment, and ask whether you should find a new time. No reproach. Some customers went quiet precisely because they felt embarrassed, and a friendly message gives them a way back.

Record the no-show on the customer's profile. Once a customer has a [documented history](/en/client-management), you can sensibly require a deposit on their next booking instead of guessing.

## What results to expect

Businesses that introduce a confirmation, an SMS reminder, a reschedule link and a written cancellation policy typically report no-shows falling from around 15 percent to between 5 and 8 percent. The rate does not reach zero, and it is not worth expecting it to.

The more important side effect is that the share of timely cancellations goes up. Those slots can be refilled, and that is where most of the recovered revenue comes from.
