A sole trader has a problem with appointments that a larger business does not. While working, you cannot take a call. And when you take a call, you interrupt the work. Every phone call during a service is therefore either a lost booking or a worse experience for the customer in front of you.

Which is why the decisive capability for a one-person business is the one that removes the phone call, not the one that adds reports.

## What a sole trader genuinely needs

Three elements solve most of it:

1. **A public booking link.** The customer picks a service and a free slot whenever it suits them. Roughly a third of bookings happen outside working hours, which is exactly when you could not have answered the phone.
2. **Automatic reminders.** Working alone, a no-show costs you twice, because you cannot move another customer into the empty slot.
3. **An invoice from the appointment.** If you build the invoice separately, you enter the same service twice.

Most of what booking products offer beyond this is, for one person, just more settings to configure.

## What a sole trader usually does not need

When comparing products, it helps to know what not to pay for until you have a team:

- managing multiple calendars and assigning staff,
- access levels and roles,
- modules for multiple business premises,
- advanced staff productivity reporting,
- product stock management, if you only sell services.

Products priced per user are generally favourable for sole traders, because you pay for a plan with one user included. How to total up the real cost is covered in the article on [what booking software costs](/en/blog/how-much-does-booking-software-cost).

## What about invoices and fiscal verification

Here there is a distinction between two kinds of sole trader that matters more than size:

- **If you take payment on site**, in cash or by card, you need a solution for [fiscal verification of invoices](/en/blog/fiscal-verification-of-invoices-slovenia). This applies to a single hairdresser with a single card terminal.
- **If you invoice every engagement and are paid by bank transfer**, verification does not apply. This is the common case for consultants, translators and tutors.

That distinction determines whether you need a fiscal cash register add-on, and it is worth five minutes with your accountant before you choose software.

## Setting up working hours when you work alone

With one person, working hours are the only source of availability, so three settings matter more than they would for a team:

**Buffer time between appointments.** Working alone, there is nobody else to tidy up. If a service is 60 minutes of work, enter 70 or 75. Without this you will be running late by the afternoon.

**Minimum notice before a booking.** Set it so a customer cannot book less than two or three hours ahead. A booking that lands mid-service for a slot half an hour later is a problem, not revenue.

**Blocked parts of the day.** Enter lunch, supply deliveries, an admin hour. A calendar that offers a slot while you are writing invoices will force you into manual cancellations.

## Absences and holidays

A business with employees can keep taking bookings while one person is away. A sole trader cannot. So entering holidays in advance, and far enough in advance, matters more for you. Enter a holiday only a week ahead and you will be cancelling appointments, which is the most expensive kind of cancellation because you are the one making it.

A practical rule: the day you book a holiday, enter the absence in your calendar.

## Where to publish the link

A booking link is only useful where customers find it. For a sole trader, three places are enough:

1. **Your social profile**, in the link field. For service businesses this is the most common source of bookings.
2. **Your email signature**, one line reading "Book an appointment".
3. **Your Google Business Profile**, where the booking link has its own field.

If you have a website, add a button in the header, not just on the contact page.

## When a sole trader becomes a team

Your first employee or contractor is the moment requirements change. That is when you need separate working hours, service assignment per person, and visibility into who delivered which appointment. It is worth choosing software that supports this even if you do not use it yet; switching products costs more than a plan that grows with you.

## Your first week, step by step

Day one, enter services with real durations and buffer time. Day two, turn on the confirmation and the reminder. Day three, publish the link in the three places listed above. Day four, book as a customer yourself and check what arrives. Day five, enter your holidays for the next six months.
