In a small psychology practice, booking can involve phone calls, email, personal calendars and billing tools. Even a team of two or three providers may spend more time than expected coordinating appointments. A public booking form is not always the answer: some practices prefer the team to arrange the first appointment directly.

## 1. Define the appointment types you offer

List appointments such as an introductory orientation call, individual counselling and online sessions. Define the duration, eligible providers, format and rescheduling rules for each. The first contact and follow-up appointments may need different processes.

## 2. Configure availability per provider

A useful calendar accounts for each psychologist's working hours, leave, existing appointments and any shared rooms. With several providers, check both individual views and appropriate team-level visibility.

Test a realistic situation: two providers, one shared room and appointments of different lengths. A visually attractive calendar is not enough if it can double-book a constrained resource.

## 3. Choose how the first appointment is arranged

| Model | When it can work |
| --- | --- |
| Staff-managed booking | A conversation or suitability check is needed first |
| Client self-booking | Appointment types are clear and suitable times can be published |
| Hybrid | First contact is arranged personally, later visits are organised in the calendar |

Let professional workflows determine the booking model. You do not need to make every service available through a public form.

## 4. Define reminders and cancellation rules

Decide who confirms appointments, when a client may reschedule or cancel, and how reminders are sent. Reminder wording should carry only the information necessary to organise the visit. Avoid diagnoses, reasons for seeking help and other sensitive details.

## 5. Separate administrative and clinical records

The purpose of an appointment calendar, contact record or invoice is different from that of clinical notes. Minimise the information collected for scheduling and specify which team members need access. Keep legally required clinical records in a suitable dedicated system, and review lawful bases and retention periods for your actual services. See Calendra's [privacy documentation](/en/legal).

## A three-day trial configuration

**Day one:** map the way appointments are actually agreed. Separate first-time enquiries, repeat visits and online meetings. For each, identify who sends confirmation, who may make changes and whether a preliminary conversation is necessary. Record only the information required to organise a meeting. If the booking form offers an open-text field, ask whether you need that field at all.

**Day two:** configure two provider calendars. Add leave, breaks and any shared room constraints, then test whether a client can be assigned only to a genuinely available slot. Try cancelling and rescheduling a booking. Check that changes become visible to the appropriate staff members, without automatically granting the entire organisation unrestricted access to all client information.

**Day three:** send test notifications to your own addresses. Review the subject lines, wording and rescheduling links. Use an administrative phrase such as “Reminder about your appointment”, rather than the reason for the session. Decide who is responsible for acting when someone replies that they cannot attend, and what happens if the usual administrator is absent.

## Write down the internal rules

Even a small team benefits from a short operational checklist: who can enter a booking, what happens when a first-contact enquiry comes in, who may approve an exception to a cancellation policy, and how duplicate requests are resolved. Make this guidance available to staff, rather than assuming that everyone interprets an online calendar in the same way.

Also prepare for temporary internet or system outages. Who contacts the client when an appointment must be confirmed urgently? How do you avoid creating two bookings for one time when you restore normal operations? Clear contingency rules are more useful than an improvised mix of personal messaging accounts and paper notes.

## How to evaluate the first week

Do not assess the system only by the number of online bookings. Count the calls needed to arrange an appointment, the minutes spent on manual reminders, and the changes caused by overlapping staff availability. Ask whether everyone can find the schedule they need without maintaining parallel spreadsheets.

A well-configured system should support the way the practice works rather than force every client into one process.
